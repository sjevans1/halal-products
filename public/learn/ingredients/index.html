01 / THE ESSENTIAL RULE

One ingredient name
is rarely the whole story.

Halal evaluation may involve the source, processing aids, manufacturing equipment, storage and traceability, not just the list on a packet. This is an educational checklist, not a certification, fatwa or personalized religious ruling.

02 / LOOK UP A LABEL TERM

Explore the details

14 topics to explore

Animal sources

Gelatin / gelatine

Gelatin can be derived from different animal sources, and a generic label may not establish which animal or processing method was used.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

Which animal or alternative source was used, and is its certification valid for this formulation?

Ask for species, supplier and processing evidence. Do not assume all gelatin has the same status.

Animal sources

Animal fats, shortening & lard

Animal fats can come from different species and processes. Pork-derived lard is not acceptable under the referenced halal guidance.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

Is the fat plant-based or animal-derived? If animal-derived, what species and verified sourcing?

Read the actual supplier declaration; do not treat generic ‘animal fat’ as a verified halal ingredient.

Animal sources

Meat stock, extracts & broths

An animal's species name does not establish how the meat or its derivatives were obtained or handled.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

Does the source meat have applicable halal verification, and are processing and shared equipment controlled?

Check the actual meat supplier and certificate scope, not just the flavour description.

Animal sources

Collagen

Collagen often originates from animal tissue; the actual source matters for suitability.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

Which species, supplier and processing route supplied this collagen?

Products with the same ingredient name may have different sourcing histories.

Processing aids & additives

Rennet

Rennet or related enzymes may be sourced from animals, microbes or other processes.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

What is the enzyme's source and was the whole production chain assessed?

The label ‘vegetarian cheese’ or ‘rennet’ alone does not document all halal criteria.

Processing aids & additives

Food processing enzymes

Processing enzymes can originate from different organisms, culture media and handling methods.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

What is the enzyme source and, where relevant, its fermentation medium and downstream process?

A processing aid may not be obvious on a finished food's ingredient panel.

Processing aids & additives

Glycerin / glycerol

Glycerol may be vegetable, synthetic or animal-derived; the label often does not disclose which.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

Is the glycerol plant-based, synthetic or animal-derived, and what documentation supports it?

Do not infer the origin solely from its additive or E-number.

Processing aids & additives

Mono- and diglycerides

Certain food emulsifiers may be derived from vegetable oils or animal fats, depending on formulation.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

Are the fatty acids vegetable or animal-derived, and is there supporting supplier evidence?

E-numbers identify an additive class, not necessarily its biological source.

Flavours & fermentation

Natural and artificial flavours

Broad flavour descriptors do not reveal every carrier, extraction solvent, or processing aid.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

Does the flavour use animal-derived inputs or alcohol-based carriers? What evidence covers the final product?

A natural flavour is not automatically a verified halal flavour.

Flavours & fermentation

Vanilla and other extracts

An extract may involve solvents or processing conditions that matter to halal assessment.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

Which solvents and extraction processes were used, and what does the relevant certifier say?

Interpretation and accepted treatment can differ; consult a qualified authority for a ruling.

Flavours & fermentation

Fermented foods & condiments

Fermented foods vary in manufacture, ingredients and possible alcohol-related processing considerations.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

What are the production ingredients and processing details, and is any halal assessment documented?

Do not categorize an entire fermented-food group without product-specific evidence.

Processing aids & additives

Whey and processed dairy

Milk-derived ingredients may also be connected with enzymes, additives and manufacturing aids.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

Are rennet, enzymes and any processing aids documented for this specific supply chain?

An ingredient may be dairy-based yet require further production checks.

Production & traceability

Halal marks & certificates

A printed mark, especially on concept packaging, is not independent proof of a current certificate. Its issuer, validity, product/facility scope and authenticity matter.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

Who issued the certificate, what does it cover, when does it expire, and can the issuer confirm it?

Tayyib's own packaging concepts are not evidence of commercial certification.

Production & traceability

Shared equipment & cross-contact

Ingredients alone may not reveal production, storage and transport practices that can affect halal suitability.

What should I ask? ＋
Ask the supplier or certifier

Were preparation equipment, storage and transport reviewed for the specific operation?

Follow the whole documented process; do not rely solely on a front-of-pack logo.
