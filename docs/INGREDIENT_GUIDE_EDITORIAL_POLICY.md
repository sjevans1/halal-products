# Tayyib Halal — ingredient guide editorial standard (LEARN-01)

**Status:** source-linked educational content candidate. It is not a halal certification product, fatwa, ingredient test, product-label scanner, or substitute for a certifier or qualified scholar.

## Purpose

A Jamaican visitor who encounters a confusing ingredient name should learn **what source or process questions to ask** rather than receive a misleading binary halal/haram answer. Support people already practicing Islam as well as beginners. Keep the language welcoming but do not flatten genuine differences in religious interpretation.

The first 14 topics in `hub/ingredient-guide.json` reference the Jamaica Halal Council's [Halal Food General Guide](https://jamhalco.org/resource/halal-food-general-guide/), including animal origins, processing aids, enzymes, additives, packaging, food-safety and traceability. This is a reading source, not a representation that the Council reviewed, endorses or certified Tayyib. Source reviewed for editorial preparation on 2026-10-15; expert validation is still outstanding.

## Safe publication rules

- **Never classify a branded product as certified or approved based solely on matching an ingredient name.** The origin, process, facility, packaging and valid certifier scope can matter independently.
- Use information states such as **source needs checking**, **supplier verification needed** or **certificate scope unknown**. A named additive and an E-number often do not uniquely identify biological origin.
- Allow qualified and dated religious or halal authority reviews to improve the guidance, with the reviewer and source recorded through a reviewed GitHub PR. Recognize that halal interpretations and accepted certifiers may vary.
- Do not create testimonials or business claims. No invented supplier/certifier, expiry, dietary claim, medical claim, ingredients, unit price or retail availability.
- Preserve links to primary sources and the original update timestamp. Re-review content on meaningful standards/source changes and at least as part of the regular editorial maintenance cadence.
- Before public indexing, a reviewer from the Muslim community and a competent halal food/certification specialist should inspect source claims, sensitivity, Arabic text and wording for overstatement.
- No uploading food-label images or customer data in the first release; the guide is entirely client-side and does not retain searches or checklist ticks.

## Golden paths and negative cases

1. Visitor finds the Learning Centre and opens `/learn/ingredients/`.
2. Search `E471`, `gelatin`, `rennet` or `natural flavour` and receives a **specific question to ask**, not a certification verdict.
3. Category filters, expanded explanation, supplier checklist and copy action work by keyboard and touch. No account is required.
4. No result explicitly suggests consulting the supplier or certificate issuer.
5. Screen readers receive accessible labels, current counts, native details, checklist status and a non-deceptive copy-success or fallback message.
6. No tracking, uploads, orders, automatic dietary classification or server/API requirement is added.

## Expansion (future separate packages)

- Editorially reviewed source cards and multilingual explanatory content with qualified Arabic and Islamic review.
- Search of **verified, current** third-party certification directories where licensed, with issuer, scope, date and dispute information visible.
- Community nutrition/cooking content with dietetic or food safety expertise where necessary.
- Never turn this checklist into a promise that a food is lawful for an individual without the necessary evidence and human authority.
